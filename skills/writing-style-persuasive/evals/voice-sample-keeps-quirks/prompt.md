Write a 350–450 word persuasive note to parents about reading aloud with children, using the sample below as the writer's voice. Keep the sample's plain vocabulary, short asides, occasional em dash, and gently self-questioning tone. Do not imitate a named writer. Do not invent research or statistics. Return only the note.

Voice sample:

I used to think reading aloud was mostly about getting through the book. One chapter, then another. If we had time, I would add a voice for the fox.

That was too narrow. The child is listening, yes, but she is also watching what we do with a quiet room. Do we hurry past the sad part? Do we stop when a question comes? Those small choices teach something, even when nobody announces the lesson.

I still rush sometimes. The difference is that I notice it now. And once you notice a habit, you can make a little room around it.

Task: persuade parents to keep a small, regular reading-aloud ritual even on busy days. Make the advice realistic rather than idealized.
